Drift Science

A Unified Structural Theory of Change Across Inference, Identity, Culture, AI, and Socio‑Technical Systems

Drift Science is a structural theory built on one principle: no system can maintain perfect identity across time.
Every system — human, cultural, algorithmic, institutional, or AI — deforms as it persists.
This deformation is drift.

The six papers in this repository form a complete architecture:

• Inference‑level drift
• Structural drift inside systems
• Internet‑scale drift
• Socio‑technical cascades
• Human intimacy drift
• Formal drift geometry, operators, and dynamics


This README maps the entire framework.


1. Overview

Drift Science explains why:

• AI models hallucinate
• cultures shift
• identities evolve
• markets destabilise
• relationships change
• ecosystems transform
• multi‑AI systems behave unpredictably


Across all domains, the mechanism is the same:
systems cannot preserve perfect sameness across time, so drift accumulates.

The repository contains six papers, each addressing a different layer of drift.

3. Core Principle

The Core Law of Drift

No system can remain perfectly identical across time.
Every update introduces non‑zero change.
Drift is not noise — it is the architecture of time.

This principle explains:

• why inference drifts
• why identities evolve
• why cultures shift
• why markets destabilise
• why AI systems diverge
• why ecosystems transform
• why stability is temporary
• why prediction fails
• why cascades occur


Drift is universal.


4. Drift Operators (From Paper 6)

Each operator pushes a system away from perfect sameness:

• Structural Drift — configuration updates
• Behavioural Drift — tendencies shift
• Constraint Drift — rules evolve
• Interaction Drift — systems influence each other
• Emergent Drift — new behaviours appear
• Noise Drift — randomness accumulates


Together they form Total Drift — the composite field of deformation.


5. Drift Geometry (From Paper 6)

Drift unfolds through structured landscapes:

• State Manifolds — the space of possible configurations
• Drift Basins — temporary stabilisation regions
• Drift Escape — accumulated drift exceeds basin resistance
• Curvature — bending of drift direction
• Torsion — twisting of drift trajectory
• Drift Surfaces — long‑term behavioural corridors


Geometry explains why drift produces patterns, stability, instability, and cascades.


6. Drift Dynamics (From Paper 6)

Drift has measurable motion:

• Velocity — rate of change
• Acceleration — change in velocity
• Jerk — change in acceleration (precursor to cascades)
• Pressure — external forces
• Resistance — internal stabilisers
• Energy — capacity for structural transformation


Dynamics explain why drift speeds up, slows down, or suddenly escapes.
